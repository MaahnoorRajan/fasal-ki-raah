# Potato case study: Punjab, January–February 2026

Potatoes are a candidate crop for the first Fasal Ki Raah demo. The following figures are historical reports from a market slump, not current quotes or representative farm-gate prices.

- On 29 January 2026, Rao Muhammad Ajmal Khan briefed a National Assembly committee on potato production and selling costs. The [official meeting summary](https://na.gov.pk/en/pressrelease_detail.php?id=7614) confirms the briefing. [Dunya's reporting the next day](https://dunya.com.pk/index.php/pakistan/2026-01-30/2624018) attributes a reported selling price of Rs 8/kg to him. This supports a specific example within the suggested Rs 5–10/kg range; it does not establish that entire range as an average.
- On 7 January, [Dawn reported farm-gate prices of Rs 10–15/kg in some markets](https://www.dawn.com/news/1965543) in its coverage of Punjab's potato belt. In a separate example in the same article, the president of Pakistan Kisan Ittehad cited a 60 kg cold-storage bag fetching Rs 600–700 with Rs 400 in transport costs (about Rs 6.67/kg). The route was unspecified. Do not combine the bag example and farm-gate prices as though they were one shipment.
- On 4 February, the [Ministry of National Food Security and Research](https://mnfsr.gov.pk/NewsDetail/YTM3ZjI2OGQtZWU3Zi00MjlhLWJhNWQtNWMwNWZkNjg1ZGNh) identified export disruption after the Afghanistan border closure and a domestic surplus as key context for the price decline. Cheaper freight alone would not create demand.

## Implication for the prototype

Use a clearly labelled fictional potato shipment in Punjab. Compare a farm-gate offer with the expected mandi proceeds after freight, loading, fees, and any unsold or rejected quantity. A match is useful only if a buyer and a feasible trip are available. No real corridor, freight saving, or farmer margin is established by these reports.
