# Discovery kit: one farm-to-market route

Start with one smallholder or tenant farmer and one goods-vehicle driver or owner connected to the same route. If possible, add a crop buyer or collection-point operator. Ask about a recent real shipment before explaining the app.

## Find an introduction

Ask a relative, classmate, or teacher who knows someone in a farming district. The farmer may be able to introduce the person who transported or collected their last crop. A brief call or voice note is enough. No interviews have been arranged yet.

**English:** Hi! I'm a computer science student studying how small farmers get crops to market. Do you know a farmer or a goods-vehicle driver in Sindh or Punjab who could spare ten minutes to explain how their last crop delivery worked? This is student research.

**Urdu:** السلام علیکم! میں کمپیوٹر سائنس کی طالبہ ہوں اور جاننا چاہتی ہوں کہ چھوٹے کسان اپنی فصل منڈی تک کیسے پہنچاتے ہیں۔ کیا آپ مجھے سندھ یا پنجاب میں کسی کسان یا فصل لے جانے والے ڈرائیور سے ملووا سکتے ہیں؟ مجھے صرف دس منٹ ان کے آخری تجربے کے بارے میں بات کرنی ہے۔

## Ask the farmer

1. What crop did you last sell? How many sacks, crates, or kilos did you load and sell?
2. Did you sell at the farm gate, through a collector, or at a mandi? Who arranged and paid for transport?
3. What did you receive for the sale? What did you pay separately for freight, loading, or market fees?
4. Was any produce damaged or rejected? What time did it need to arrive?
5. What might make a shared trip impractical? How and in what language would you prefer to book?

## Ask the driver

1. Which market trips repeat, and on which leg is there actually unused space?
2. How many extra kilos and sacks or crates can you safely carry? What cargo can you accept?
3. Who can approve an extra load? How much time can you spend picking up and unloading?
4. How would you quote the job, confirm delivery, and handle a delay or rejected load?

If you reach a buyer or coordinator, ask about receiving hours, packaging, quality checks, and whether small loads can be pooled at one collection point.

## Choose the first test

Pick one district, one crop, and one route only after finding a real transport need and plausible spare vehicle capacity. Compare with the farmer's current arrangement, including a farm-gate sale if relevant. Measure transport cost per kg and the farmer's net sale proceeds before production costs. The interviews can guide a prototype; they cannot establish national effects on food prices or farmer profits.
